% ═══ WHY FORM ═══
\subsection*{Why FORM}

What drew me to this call specifically is your instrument: the Computer Science Library (CSLib) in Lean, with FORM as a founding player in its technical leadership. Lean 4 is my primary proof assistant, and I already work on the two problems a universal, AI-assisted repository must solve: generating formalizations at scale, and guaranteeing they are \emph{faithful} rather than merely well-typed. FORM's vision of combining human and machine effort for construction that is both rapid and reliable is, almost word for word, the problem statement of my current research. The breadth of the ACP section (theorem provers, type systems, programming languages, distributed systems) is also exactly where a researcher who bridges mathematical foundations and AI tooling can contribute across projects rather than in a single niche.

% ═══ WHAT I BRING ═══
\subsection*{What I bring}

Through the \textbf{Apart Research Secure Program Synthesis Fellowship (2026)}, I am building \textbf{BabelBench}, a benchmark that measures whether LLM translations of theorems across formal systems preserve logical meaning. It spans Lean 4 and Coq on the type-theoretic side and TLA+ and Dafny on the model-theoretic side (the specification and verification tools of the distributed and software systems FORM aims to formalize). Crucially, fidelity is judged by real proof verifiers and the Z3 solver, never by another language model. Designing these metrics forced me to work hands-on across four formalisms and to take seriously how easily a translation can typecheck while quietly changing what a statement means.

That concern is the core of my main research project, \textbf{AViD Journal}, an end-to-end AI-assisted autoformalization pipeline. It ingests research papers, formalizes their results in Lean 4, and checks novelty against Mathlib along three axes: existence, non-triviality, and structural proof-distance. Its central finding is blunt: \textit{fidelity \(\neq\) compilation}. A formalization can pass the kernel and still fail to mean the original theorem. For any repository that intends to hold formalized computing at scale, that gap between ``it compiles'' and ``it is faithful'' \emph{is} the problem, and I have spent a year building tooling to measure and narrow it.

On the foundational side, I am formalizing my Licenciatura thesis (Priestley duality for bounded distributive lattices with normal operators) in Lean 4, and my research background is in algebraic logic. That gives me the theoretical footing FORM values (logic, type systems, categories) alongside the systems-building experience above.

% ═══ WHAT I WOULD PURSUE ═══
\subsection*{What I would pursue at FORM}

Concretely, I would bring these two threads directly into CSLib. On one side, an AI-assisted formalization workflow that helps populate the library faster; on the other, a fidelity layer, built on real verifiers and Z3 in the spirit of BabelBench rather than on LLM judgment, that certifies a machine-generated contribution actually captures its informal specification before it enters the repository. The failure modes I have catalogued in autoformalization are precisely the ones that would otherwise erode trust in a library built partly by machines. I would also be glad to push the cross-paradigm side of BabelBench toward FORM's systems work, connecting TLA+ and Dafny specifications to their Lean counterparts with verified fidelity. Equally, I would be happy to shape this within one of FORM's existing projects.

My CV, code, and project repositories are available at \href{https://ayrtonporto.github.io}{ayrtonporto.github.io}. I would welcome the chance to discuss where this fits best within FORM. I am available to begin immediately and can relocate on short notice; the late-2026 timeline also suits me well.
